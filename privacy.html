darkmoon — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 September 2026

The short version

darkmoon runs on your computer. Your photographs, your edits, your catalogue and your presets stay there. We do not collect them, we do not see them, and we have no server for them to go to.

There is no account to make, no analytics, no crash reporting, no usage tracking and no update check. The application does not contact us at all.

One feature sends an image off your machine, and only when you turn it on yourself: Cloud AI. Section 3 covers exactly what it sends and to whom.

1. Who we are

Vinicius Oliveira, the author of darkmoon. Contact: viniciusfos1996@gmail.com.

2. What stays on your computer

Everything the application produces lives on your own disk, under your documents folder, in darkmoon/:

What Where
The catalogue of folders and photographs you have opened darkmoon_catalog.json
Your settings darkmoon_settings.json
Your edits, per photograph darkmoon_photo_curves.json, darkmoon_photo_presets.json, darkmoon_photo_meta.json
Masks and object removals darkmoon_masks.json, darkmoon_inpaint.json
Thumbnails, previews and AI caches darkmoon/previews/, and the AI cache folders beside it

Your original photographs are never modified. The application reads them and writes only the files you explicitly export.

You can delete any of this at any time; the application rebuilds what it needs. Deleting the darkmoon/ folder resets it to a fresh install.

3. Cloud AI — the one thing that leaves your machine

The AI Denoise window has a Cloud AI tab. It is off by default. To use it you have to pick a provider and paste an API key that you obtained from that provider yourself.

When you then run it on a photograph:

  • the image is uploaded to the provider you chose — Topaz Labs, OpenAI or Google, depending on your selection — together with your API key, so that they can process it and return a result;
  • that transfer goes directly from your computer to them. It does not pass through us, and we never receive a copy;
  • what they do with the image, how long they keep it, and whether they train on it are governed by their terms and privacy policy, not by this one. Read them before sending photographs of other people.

Every other AI feature in darkmoon — denoising, upscaling, masking, colourising, object removal, generative fill — runs entirely on your own machine, on models that ship inside the application. Nothing about them touches the network.

4. Your API keys

Keys you enter are stored encrypted at rest using the Windows Data Protection API (CryptProtectData), which ties them to your Windows account on that machine: another account, and another computer, cannot read them. They are sent to nobody except the provider they belong to.

You can remove a key by clearing the field in the Cloud AI tab.

5. What we collect

Nothing.

To be concrete rather than reassuring: the only code in darkmoon that opens a network connection is the Cloud AI providers described in section 3. There is no analytics library, no crash reporter, no telemetry, no licence check and no update check in the application.

If that ever changes — a licence activation, for example, would have to contact a server — this document will be updated before the version that does it ships, and the change will be described in the release notes.

6. Children

darkmoon is a photo editor for general use. It is not directed at children and we knowingly collect no data from anyone, of any age.

7. Your rights

Since we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to show you, correct or delete. Data protection law — including the GDPR, where it applies — gives you rights over data a company holds about you; in our case the honest answer is that the set is empty.

For the photographs you choose to send through Cloud AI, the rights you have are against that provider, and you exercise them with that provider.

8. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy. The version that ships inside a release of darkmoon is the one that applies to that release, and the date at the top says when it was last changed.
